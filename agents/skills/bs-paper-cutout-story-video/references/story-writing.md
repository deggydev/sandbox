# Writing the story

The paper style makes anything feel like a picture book. The writing has to
match: short, concrete, warm, one idea per scene.

## Shape

7-11 scenes, about 35-50 seconds. A reliable arc:

1. **Title** (paper, word strips): the question or the name. 3-4 s.
2. **Hook / setting** (world): where we are, who is there. 4 s.
3. **Explanation** (2-4 scenes): the steps, causes or plot points, each
   one a concrete picture. Mix world scenes, paper close-ups and a numbered
   card or two.
4. **Overview** (steps or cards): the whole idea in 3-4 pieces.
5. **Meaning** (world or cards): why it matters, the lesson, the themes.
6. **Close** (world, often `fade` and a gentler theme): one warm line.
7. **End card**: name or topic, a handwritten tagline.

Do not copy the scene order of the examples; let the topic decide. A book
summary leans on characters and places; a science topic on a process; a
brand story on a day in the life.

## Words

- Headlines: 2-8 words per line, at most two lines.
- Body lines: at most two short lines, plain words a ten-year-old knows.
- Highlight one key phrase per scene with `**...**`.
- Facts: only well-established ones, rounded conservatively ("less than
  1%"). Health topics follow common public guidance (for example, wash
  hands with soap for at least 20 seconds).
- Book and film summaries: retell in your own words; never reproduce long
  passages or famous lines verbatim.

## Turning the topic into pictures

For every scene ask: what single picture would a child cut out to show
this? Use that as the main prop, then add one or two supporting props and
the world around it. Examples:

| idea | picture |
|---|---|
| water comes from rain and snow | rain clouds over mountains |
| water is cleaned | the waterworks with a smiling drop |
| germs travel on hands | a door, a hand, small germs on both |
| a fox wants to be tamed | the fox and the boy in a wheat field |
| bread is baked early | a shop at dawn, a bicycle arriving |

Keep the default green world (sun, clouds, trees) unless the story happens
somewhere else; switch theme only for a real change of place or mood.
